Fig. 3 + 4 · shoulder actuator

How the cycloidal drive gets 20:1

The motor spins an eccentric cam. The disc rolls around a ring of 21 fixed pins, and because it has only 20 lobes it walks back one lobe per motor turn: 20 motor turns = 1 output turn, in the opposite direction. Pins through holes in the disc pick off that slow rotation and drive the output flange, which runs in the 6806 bearing.

Sequence

One turn, start to finish

  1. Makes a moveThen presses the "done" button, which avoids guessing when a hand has left the board.
  2. Reads the boardCompares the new frame with the last one, square by square, and finds which squares changed.
  3. Checks the moveMatches the changes against legal moves. If none fits, the OLED asks for a fix.
  4. Picks a replyAt the difficulty set in the menu.
  5. Plans the motionCapture first: carry the taken piece to the tray. Then the move itself, with castling and promotion as special cases.
  6. Moves the armHover, descend, grip, lift to carry height, travel, descend, release.
  7. ParksBack to a pose outside the camera's view, so the next photo is clean.
Decisions · rev B

How the open questions were settled

  • Elbow fold near the base. Rank 8 needs the forearm to close to about 26° from the upper arm. The elbow is offset: the forearm runs beside the upper arm, so the links pass each other instead of colliding.
  • Far-corner margin. The shoulder axis moves up from 12 to 18 cm. With the longer roll wrist (15 cm to the fingertips), a1 at carry height needs 92% of full reach, with no torque cost. A 30 cm forearm is the fallback.
  • Shoulder torque. Long-body NEMA 17 (~0.65 Nm), 20:1, plus a spring counterbalance. The elbow motor sits behind the shoulder axis as a counterweight. 30:1 is the fallback.
  • Fifth motor. Wrist roll (J5), so the fingers can line up with crowded pieces and the arm is useful for other jobs later. Six motors and six drivers in total.
  • Homing. An AS5600 magnetic angle sensor on each joint output gives the absolute angle at power-up and catches skipped steps. The gripper homes against a switch.
